# Alfa Start 360

A product concept for young entrepreneurs, developed as part of the Alfa Championship hackathon.

---

## About the Project

**Alfa Start 360** is a concept for a service designed for young entrepreneurs and self-employed individuals.

The idea is to help early-stage businesses solve several common problems in one place:

- accepting payments;
- finding their first customers;
- finding contractors;
- simplifying accounting and tax-related tasks.

The target audience is young entrepreneurs aged 17–25, including small cafés, Beauty professionals, Digital freelancers, and other micro-businesses.

---

## The Problem

Starting a small business usually means dealing with several separate tasks:

- setting up payment processing;
- finding the first customers;
- finding employees or contractors;
- dealing with taxes and accounting;
- managing cash flow.

**Project hypothesis:** some of these tasks can be combined within a single banking ecosystem.

---

## The Solution

The concept combines several directions.

### Payments

Using existing banking tools such as AlfaPOS and SBP to help small businesses start accepting payments with less friction.

### Alfa Radar

A service for helping businesses find their first customers among the bank's users.

For example, a small café could offer a special deal to students nearby.

### Alfa Shift

A service for finding contractors for short-term tasks.

The main idea is to help small businesses find suitable people faster.

### AI Accounting

A concept for automating part of routine accounting tasks using transaction data.

---

## Why It Could Be Interesting for a Bank

A bank can interact with a business not only through a bank account or acquiring services, but also through additional products.

The concept also creates a connection between two user groups:

**Entrepreneurs** → get access to customers and contractors.

**Students** → get local business offers and potential part-time opportunities.

This creates a potential network effect between the two sides of the ecosystem.

## What I Reconsidered After the Project

After the hackathon, I reviewed the concept from the perspective of real-world constraints rather than just the initial idea.

### 1. Legal Risks

The initial concept included using civil-law contracts for regular shifts.

After reviewing this idea, it became clear that such a model could create a risk of employment relationship reclassification.

A real product would therefore require a separate legal model and verification of the contractor's status.

### 2. Cashback Economics

The initial concept included a high welcome cashback to attract students.

After reconsidering the idea, it became clear that this mechanism could be expensive for the bank, especially with a low average transaction value, while not necessarily leading to long-term retention.

For further development, I would compare different acquisition mechanics and measure **CAC, LTV, and repeat purchases**.

### 3. Data Security

Using external LLMs to process banking and accounting data would create serious security and confidentiality requirements.

For a real banking product, such a solution would require a secure infrastructure and a separate information security assessment.

---

## What I Would Validate Next

If I continued developing the project as a real product, I would start by testing the following hypotheses:

1. Do early-stage entrepreneurs actually need a single service for these tasks?
2. Which features provide the most value to them?
3. Would students use local business offers through a banking app?
4. What is the acquisition cost for entrepreneurs and users?
5. Which scenarios lead to repeat usage?

---

## Result

The result is a concept that combines **payments, customer acquisition, and contractor search** for early-stage businesses.

For me, the project was useful not only as a hackathon submission but also as a practical product analytics exercise.

After completing it, I was able to critically review my own decisions, identify legal, economic, and technical limitations, and define what would need to be validated before further development.
